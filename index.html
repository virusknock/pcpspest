Typical Features in Pest Control Methods We Follow

Chemical Treatments

  • Pesticides and Insecticides: These are chemical substances used to kill or repel pests. They can be applied in various forms like sprays, baits, dusts, or aerosols.
  • Action: They directly target specific pests, either by poisoning, suffocating, or disrupting the pest's biological functions (such as nervous system or reproduction).
  • Barrier Creation: Some chemical treatments are used to create a barrier around the property, preventing pests from entering.
  • Effectiveness: Highly effective for immediate or large-scale pest infestations, but may have risks to the environment, pets, or humans if not applied correctly.

Biological Control

  • Natural Predators: This involves introducing or encouraging the growth of natural predators or parasites that can reduce the pest population.
  • Example: Releasing ladybugs to control aphids or using nematodes to target soil-borne pests.
  • Bacterial and Fungal Agents: Using naturally occurring bacteria, fungi, or viruses that infect and kill specific pests.
  • Example: Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt), a bacterium that targets caterpillars in agriculture.
  • Eco-friendly: Biological control is often used as a more environmentally friendly, non-toxic alternative to chemical pesticides.
  • Sustainability: It can offer long-term pest management without harming the ecosystem.

Traps

  • Physical Traps: Traps are used to capture or kill pests without chemicals. Types of traps include
  • Rodent traps: Snap traps, live traps, and glue boards for mice and rats.
  • Insect traps: Sticky traps or light traps for catching flying insects like flies, moths, or mosquitoes.
  • Pheromone traps: These use synthetic chemicals to mimic the natural pheromones of pests (like moths) to lure them into traps.
  • Eco-friendly: Biological control is often used as a more environmentally friendly, non-toxic alternative to chemical pesticides.
  • Humane Traps: Non-lethal traps are used for capturing and releasing animals like squirrels, raccoons, and other wildlife.
  • Monitoring: Traps can also serve as monitoring tools to detect pest activity.

Exclusion and Barriers

  • Sealing Entry Points: This method involves identifying and sealing potential entry points where pests can get inside a building, such as cracks, gaps in windows, doors, or vents.
  • Physical Barriers: Using mesh, screens, or other physical materials to block access to certain areas (e.g., attic vents, crawl spaces).
  • Structural Repairs: Repairing damaged walls, roofs, or foundations that might allow pests to enter.
  • Preventative Maintenance: Regularly inspecting the property for signs of pests and sealing any potential entry points before an infestation begins.
  • Long-Term Solution: This method prevents future infestations and can be combined with other pest control methods for comprehensive management.
  • Exclusion and barriers: Sealing entry points and preventing pests from gaining access to a property.
